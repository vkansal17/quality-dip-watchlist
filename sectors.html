The six cross-cutting forces

Geopolitics / oil shock — US–Israeli war with Iran, ~9th month
WTI settled at $103.51/bbl and Brent at $117.02 on Friday 23 Oct, Brent's highest since April and up more than 85% in 2026 after Houthi strikes on Saudi Red Sea infrastructure (Yanbu) and Saudi Aramco's emergency evacuation of its Dhahran HQ. Diesel is the acute pain point: US on-highway diesel averaged $6.20/gal on 5 Oct (+67% year-on-year), and regular gasoline was $4.29/gal on 23 Oct.
Inflation re-acceleration + interest rates
September CPI rose 3.7% y/y (core 2.6%) with gasoline contributing more than half the monthly rise, and PPI rose 6.3% y/y; the Fed raised rates 25bp on 16 September to 3.75–4.00%, its first hike since 2023. The 10-year Treasury touched 5.35% on 7 Oct (highest since 2002) and closed 23 Oct at 5.00%; 94 of 100 economists in a Reuters survey expect a hold at the 28 Oct FOMC, with a further hike widely priced for December.
A strong-but-two-speed economy
S&P Global flash composite PMI was 57.6 in October (down from September's five-year high of 58.4), with new orders at 58.2 — highest since March 2022 — but output prices at 59.8, the fastest rise since August 2022. Consensus Q3 GDP is ~3.0% annualised, mainly driven by consumer spending and business investment in AI, with a warning that oil-driven inflation is likely to slow consumer spending in Q4.
A cooling labour market underneath the boom
September nonfarm payrolls rose just +29,000 (vs. 84–90k expected), unemployment ticked up to 4.2%, July was revised to −10,000, and average hourly earnings growth slowed to +3.0% y/y. Financial activities employment is down 129,000 since May 2025, including 90,000 at insurance carriers — even as PMI factory employment hit its highest since March 2012.
Tariffs and trade-policy whiplash
The US imposed 50% tariffs on ~$20bn of Canadian goods over the summer under Section 338 (on top of Section 232 duties) after the USMCA/CUSMA extension failed, and Canada retaliated dollar-for-dollar (15–50%). On 23 Oct, days before the midterms, the White House exempted some products (plywood, some paper, machinery, refrigeration equipment, packing crates — effective 30 Oct) while adding others (carpets, paints, shampoos, cocoa powder, air-conditioning, valves, waste products).
Midterm politics — 3 November, Democratic sweep priced in
Prediction markets on 20 Oct put Democratic control of the House at 0.915 and the Senate at 0.645 (Polymarket); deVere put a Democratic sweep at close to two-in-three and a Republican sweep at barely one-in-ten. Markets are already trading it — defence stocks and banks/AI are being sold on expected Democratic scrutiny — while housing affordability, farm diesel costs and gasoline prices have become central campaign issues.

#1 · AI hyperscalers, data-centre builders & the power chain serving them Negative Severe

Demand for AI compute is still booming, but the financing model is what broke: hyperscaler capex is outrunning cash flow at 5% interest rates, forcing debt issuance and new share classes, and the market is repricing the whole chain — from Meta and CoreWeave to the utilities meant to power the build-out.

Market Inflation & rates Economy Politics AI capex

Evidence:

  • Meta (22 Oct earnings): Q3 revenue $64.6bn, +26% y/y, beating estimates — yet shares fell ~7% on the day, ~9.4% over five sessions; capex was ~$34.9bn in the quarter, full-year guidance was raised to $135–145bn, and free cash flow swung to −$2.1bn, its first negative quarter since the 2012 IPO.
  • CoreWeave fell ~25% in one week (with ~$50bn+ debt; a July 2026 bond issued at 9.75% was trading at a ~16% yield by 14 Oct); on 22 Oct the Nasdaq fell 2.60% to 26,008.86 — its worst session since June — despite earnings beats.
  • Utilities were the worst S&P 500 sector in Q3, −12.38% (Q3 sector returns from a single syndicated series citing S&P data), even as data-centre electricity demand is projected to grow ~24% a year to 2030.
  • Bloom Energy raised 2026 revenue guidance a third time (to $4.4–4.6bn; Q3 revenue +138% to $1.24bn) and still closed −11.24% on 23 Oct; Meta also announced a new non-voting Class C share class (effective 19 Jan 2027) to help finance the build-out, and its 5-year credit-default swaps hit record highs.
  • Bank of America research cited in coverage put AI return on incremental invested capital below its cost of capital.

Watch next: Alphabet/Microsoft earnings 28 Oct and Amazon 29 Oct — cloud growth vs. capex and any FCF/AI-debt guidance — plus the 28 Oct Fed decision's effect on AI borrowing costs.

#2 · Oil & gas, refiners & energy services Positive Severe

The clearest beneficiary of the current environment: war-driven oil prices (Brent up more than 85% in 2026) and wide diesel crack spreads are delivering windfall earnings and the best sector performance of the year — though the report cautions the gains are headline-driven and volatile.

Geopolitics / oil Economy Inflation & rates Politics

Evidence:

  • WTI settled at $103.51 and Brent at $117.02 on 23 Oct, Brent's highest since April; Energy (XLE) rose +2.60% on 23 Oct alone, leading the S&P 500, with oil & gas drilling +5.80% and field services +4.96% that day.
  • Energy returned +16.48% in Q3 — by far the best sector (Q3 sector returns from a single syndicated series citing S&P data).

  • Caveat: Exxon itself flagged earlier in the war that disrupted cargoes and derivative timing effects can cut reported profit in a given quarter, and a ceasefire headline could reverse $5–8/bbl in a session.

Watch next: Exxon and Chevron Q3 results on 30 Oct, the 28 Oct EIA petroleum report, and any US/Israel decision on renewed strikes on Iran after the midterms.

#4 · Housing, homebuilders, mortgage & commercial real estate / REITs Negative Severe

The most rate-crushed part of the real economy: a 7.40% mortgage rate has pushed builder sentiment to its lowest since June 2012, while maturing commercial real estate loans cannot be refinanced at 5%+ Treasury yields, driving CMBS distress to its highest since February 2013.

Inflation & rates Geopolitics / oil Trade / tariffs Jobs Politics

Evidence:

  • NAHB/Wells Fargo homebuilder sentiment fell to 29 in October — lowest since June 2012 — with buyer traffic at 18 (lowest since April 2012 outside the pandemic), 43% of builders cutting prices (a 2020–26 high) and 65% using incentives. [Reuters, verified live]
  • The 30-year mortgage averaged 7.40% — highest in nearly three years (7.28% on 1 Oct, from 6.34% a year earlier); more than one-third of builders reported immigration enforcement activity in their market in the past month, causing delays.
  • The FTSE Nareit All Equity REITs Index fell 5.7% in September and >8% from late August to mid-October while the S&P 500 gained ~1%; Real Estate was −6.30% in Q3.
  • CMBS special servicing hit 11.42% in August — highest since February 2013 — on >$5 trillion of outstanding CRE/multifamily mortgages, as maturing low-rate loans cannot be refinanced at 5%+ Treasury yields.
  • Even high-occupancy Realty Income fell 12.5% in two months to 9 Oct.

Watch next: Freddie Mac's weekly mortgage rate and whether the 10-year falls sustainably below 5% — builders rallied intraday on 23–24 Oct on a dip to 7.32%, showing how binary this sector now is on yields.

#6 · Defence (primes vs. smaller contractors) Mixed Severe

Record fundamentals, falling valuations: backlogs and war demand have never been stronger, but the stocks have de-rated hard on IPO supply, drone-vs-interceptor doubts, and above all midterm risk — the proposed $1.5 trillion FY27 defence budget (+~42%) needs Congress, and a Democratic Congress could trim the increase.

Geopolitics / oil Politics Inflation & rates Economy

Evidence:

  • On 20 Oct Northrop Grumman rose ~7% (its best day in over a year) on a record $109.6bn backlog ($16.4bn of Q3 awards); RTX reported a record $340bn backlog on >$81bn of awards. [Morningstar/Dow Jones, verified live]
  • The CBO estimated US operations against Iran cost ~$38bn through 1 Aug, including $21.7bn to replace expended munitions ($13.1bn of missile-defence interceptors); CSIS estimated Patriot inventories below 1,000 interceptors.
  • Yet the equal-weight SPDR Aerospace & Defense ETF is down ~21% since the start of the Iran war (iShares ITA −15% over the same span; ITA −17.86% from its 14 Aug close by 19 Oct); Lockheed Martin is −20%+ from its March peak, Northrop ~−35% and L3Harris ~−36% on one FT-cited count (before Northrop's 20 Oct bounce).
  • Analysts also cite a flood of small-defence IPOs (6 US + 5 European this year), doubts about expensive interceptors vs. cheap drones, and a >6%-of-GDP deficit at 5% rates; munitions replenishment has bipartisan support even if the budget increase does not.

Watch next: Lockheed Martin results, the 3 Nov midterm result, and whether replenishment appropriations survive — the gap between backlog and share price is the trade.

#9 · Agriculture & food Negative High

A cost shock large enough to trigger emergency federal intervention: diesel at $6.20/gal (+67% y/y) lands at the most fuel-intensive moment of the farm year, fertiliser supply is disrupted, and the policy response — an excise-tax deferral plus an $8bn aid programme — itself proves the magnitude.

Geopolitics / oil Trade / tariffs Inflation & rates Politics Economy

Evidence:

  • National on-highway diesel was $6.20/gal on 5 Oct, +67% y/y (+71% in the Midwest), after an all-time high $6.53 in September (+88% since the start of the year) (EIA data via USDA).
  • Fertiliser is the second hit: the Middle East war has disrupted about one-third of world fertiliser supply, lifting prices sharply.
  • On 6 Oct an executive order let farmers use dyed (off-road) diesel on highways and deferred the 24.4c/gal federal excise tax through 31 Dec; several governors (NC, Kentucky) matched it.
  • On 21 Oct the administration announced an $8bn 'Harvest Fuel Relief' programme via USDA/Commodity Credit Corporation — on top of a $12bn package last December for tariff-related farm losses; Canada's 50%-tariff fight also directly hits farm machinery, packaging and border-state agriculture.

Watch next: Whether diesel relief is extended past 31 Dec, fertiliser availability for spring planting, and how much of the cost reaches supermarket food prices.

#10 · Small-cap stocks (Russell 2000) Negative High

The purest listed proxy for the cost of money: small caps carry more floating-rate and bank debt and lack mega-cap free cash flow, so a 5%+ 10-year directly reprices their refinancing — driving a technical correction from the August record, and sharp relief rallies whenever yields ease.

Inflation & rates Market Economy Jobs

Evidence:

  • The Russell 2000 set a record close of 3,068.42 on 14 Aug, then slid ~9% by early October and entered a technical correction (>11% from peak) on 14 Oct, surrendering its year-to-date lead over the S&P 500.
  • Bloomberg Intelligence data cited in coverage puts the median Russell 2000 stock ~25% below its 52-week high — far worse than the index.
  • Truist CIO Keith Lerner, quoted by MarketWatch: 'The first, and most important factor… has been rising long-term Treasury yields. The higher interest rates are biting — it's just that the technology sector has masked that.'
  • In the week to 23 Oct the Russell 2000 rose +2.0%, leading all major indexes, as yields eased below 5% and money rotated out of AI — confirming small caps are now trading as a levered play on the 10-year.

Watch next: The 10-year around 5% and regional-bank credit conditions — a sustained break below 5% is the bull case; a December Fed hike likely re-opens the correction.

Why it's on this list

Down 27% from its 52-week high of $221.98, trading at 21% of its 52-week range. The fall has stabilized — 20-day momentum is +9.0% rather than still accelerating down. RSI 54 says washed out rather than euphoric, and $1,073M of average daily dollar volume makes it a large, liquid name — the dip is in the price, screened against evidence the business itself is broken. It carries a full hand-researched brief below (dip cause, business pulse, analyst consensus, risks).

What caused the dip

Two company/sector shocks: (1) a sharp drop around Q2 (Jul 23) despite raised FCF guidance, as postpaid net account adds fell 13% YoY with 0.99% churn; (2) SpaceX — the stock fell 13.2% on Oct 9, its worst day since 2013, on SpaceX's ~$8B acquisition of nationwide 800 MHz spectrum, then -7.7% on Oct 23 on reports SpaceX is seeking ~80,000 U.S. cell sites for a wireless build-out. AT&T and Verizon sold off too — sector-wide competitive repricing.

Business pulse

Operationally solid. Q2 service revenue +9% to $19.0B, postpaid service revenue +13%, revenue $22.79B; FY26 guidance reaffirmed in September and adjusted FCF guidance raised to $18.4-18.8B; dividend raised 15% on Sep 24. Q3 due Oct 28.

What analysts say

Moderate Buy consensus, average target ~$242-248 — the widest implied upside of the researched group (~+65%). JPMorgan trimmed to $260 Overweight (Sep 21); Scotiabank $212 Sector Outperform (Oct 9); Raymond James upgraded to Outperform (Oct 12, target $170 — well below peers); Bernstein Neutral, $180 (Oct 21).

Media & social buzz

More fear than dip-buying in tone — coverage is dominated by the SpaceX threat and 'more than two-year low' headlines; retail sentiment on Stocktwits turned bullish, but fewer explicit buy-the-dip features than for BKNG/MCD/CAT.

Risks & what would break the thesis

The dip driver is potentially structural — a well-capitalized Starlink Mobile entrant could end the three-carrier oligopoly economics. Add slowing subscriber growth, sector read-through risk, and leverage (debt/equity 1.48).

🔬 Research verdict

HOLD deep-researched 2026-10-25 · Dip Top 50 #10 · Pro Top 50 #21 (A)

T-Mobile's core business is intact — Q2 raised adjusted FCF guidance and service revenue is growing ~9% — but the stock has been repriced by a live competitive shock: SpaceX's $8B spectrum purchase and reported plan to lease ~80,000 cell sites as a fourth national carrier. At ~16x earnings with a double-digit FCF yield the price already reflects much fear, yet the sector leg fails while the threat is still escalating, so research says hold into the Oct 28 print rather than buy the dip outright.

Key risks:

  • SpaceX builds a real fourth carrier faster than expected, forcing price/promotion wars and margin compression from TMUS's ~22% operating margin
  • Oct 28 earnings disappoint on subscriber adds or churn after rate-plan changes
  • Leverage limits flexibility if competition forces heavier network/promotional spending

What would change this verdict: A clean Oct 28 print with firm guidance and evidence SpaceX's entry stays years away would clear the sector leg toward BUY; a guidance cut or confirmed aggressive SpaceX launch timeline would push toward SELL.

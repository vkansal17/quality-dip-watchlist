Why it's on this list

Down 11% from its 52-week high of $284.02, trading at 64% of its 52-week range. The fall has stabilized — 20-day momentum is +2.6% rather than still accelerating down. RSI 49 says washed out rather than euphoric, and $9,140M of average daily dollar volume makes it a large, liquid name — the dip is in the price, screened against evidence the business itself is broken. It carries a full hand-researched brief below (dip cause, business pulse, analyst consensus, risks).

What caused the dip

The shallowest dip of the group (-13.6% from high; only -0.7% over one month) — mostly sector-level AI-capex/FCF anxiety, including the Oct 22 Nasdaq drop as investors questioned AI data-center financing costs, rather than any Amazon-specific bad news. It rose ~3.9% on Oct 19 on an analyst upgrade.

Business pulse

The strongest recent print of the group. Q2 2026: revenue $200.6B (+20% YoY, first $200B+ quarter) vs $196.5B consensus; AWS $42.2B, +37% YoY — fastest since 2021; operating income +43%; AWS backlog ~$496B of contracted commitments. FY capex guided to ~$220B; trailing FCF is negative ~$7.6B.

What analysts say

Buy consensus — average target ~$321-332 (+30-34%) across ~70 analysts. Morgan Stanley raised its target to $350 and named AMZN a top pick (Oct 19), citing AWS acceleration into 2027-28; JPMorgan, BofA and KeyBanc hold Buys at $320-$380.

Media & social buzz

Explicit lag/value framing is common — 'AWS is running at 37% growth and this stock has no business being at $251,' pointing to ~20x trailing earnings vs a ~$324 mean target. Tone: bullish-but-patient, earnings-dependent.

Risks & what would break the thesis

Execution against a $220B capex plan with negative trailing FCF — if Oct 29 shows AWS growth at/below the low end of the 30-35% expectation, or another capex raise, the 'cheap for its growth' case weakens. Part of Q2's huge EPS beat came from non-operating Anthropic revaluation gains, so headline earnings quality is less spectacular than reported.

Why it's on this list

Down 27% from its 52-week high of $219.68, trading at 18% of its 52-week range. The fall has stabilized — 20-day momentum is +0.2% rather than still accelerating down. RSI 50 says washed out rather than euphoric, and $1,264M of average daily dollar volume makes it a large, liquid name — the dip is in the price, screened against evidence the business itself is broken. It carries a full hand-researched brief below (dip cause, business pulse, analyst consensus, risks).

What caused the dip

A year-long de-rating (-26/27% YTD): Q2 beat followed by soft Q3 guidance (growth of just 4-6%), Middle East conflict hitting long-haul travel and FX, an FTC fees probe into Priceline with a possible ~$500M bill, and the agentic-AI bypass fear — one study found AI tools send 71% of travelers to book direct. Note: a 25-for-1 split completed Apr 2, 2026.

Business pulse

Strong and cash-generative. Q2 revenue $7.35B (+8%), adjusted EPS $2.54 (+15%), gross bookings $51B (+9%), adjusted EBITDA $2.65B (+9%), FCF $3.6B (+16%), a record $4.1B returned to shareholders. FY26 consensus still calls for EPS +~15%.

What analysts say

TipRanks Strong Buy (22 Buy / 7 Hold), consensus ~$233 (+50%); MarketBeat Moderate Buy, average $233.71. Recent: BTIG Buy $250 (Oct 22), Melius $225 (Oct 20), BofA Buy $234 (Oct 15). Bears: Cantor Fitzgerald cut to $170 Neutral (Oct 12), Wells Fargo $190 'Reduce' (Oct 7).

Media & social buzz

The strongest explicit buy-the-dip framing of the researched names — Motley Fool (Oct 24): 'I would invest in Booking right now… beaten down… this won't last forever.' Insider Monkey argues it is more profitable than Airbnb at half the forward multiple with twice the FCF.

Risks & what would break the thesis

AI disintermediation may be structural, not cyclical (direct-booking shift, take-rate pressure); FTC outcome; geopolitics/oil hitting international travel; growth decelerating to mid-single digits.

🔬 Research verdict

HOLD deep-researched 2026-10-25 · Dip Top 50 #6

Booking is a cash machine at ~17x trailing earnings near its 52-week low, and its asset-light model genuinely blunts the travel sector's fuel shock — Q2 FCF hit $3.6B despite the Middle East disruption. But management has guided growth down to 4-6% into a severe-negative travel backdrop with an unresolved AI-disintermediation question: three passes, two mixed, no fails — HOLD until the Nov 3 print shows whether the deceleration bottoms.

  • Analyst opinion is genuinely split (BTIG Buy $250 vs Cantor Neutral $170) — Q4/holiday guidance on Nov 3 decides the debate

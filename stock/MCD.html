Why it's on this list

Down 27% from its 52-week high of $341.06, trading at 18% of its 52-week range. The fall has stabilized — 20-day momentum is +8.5% rather than still accelerating down. RSI 60 says washed out rather than euphoric, and $1,323M of average daily dollar volume makes it a large, liquid name — the dip is in the price, screened against evidence the business itself is broken. It carries a full hand-researched brief below (dip cause, business pulse, analyst consensus, risks).

What caused the dip

Company-specific U.S. slump — shares -22.9% in six months on weak U.S. comps, a value menu that missed, poor marketing/execution and negative guest counts, plus investor concern over the $8.5B 'McDonald's > NEXT' plan (Sep 23 Investor Day) slowing unit growth and lifting capex.

Business pulse

Slowing but still growing and profitable. Q2 global comps +1.3%, U.S. comps +0.8% (traffic negative, check positive), revenue $7.10B (+3.7%), EPS $3.38 vs $3.32 consensus. The NEXT plan targets low-to-mid-50% operating margin by 2030. Q3 due Nov 4.

What analysts say

Moderate Buy consensus, average target $295.94 (+19%) — but October was a wave of cuts: Bernstein $295->$250 Market Perform, Guggenheim $290->$250 Neutral, Wells Fargo $300->$270 Overweight, KeyCorp $305->$280 Overweight. Lone Sell: Melius at $230.

Media & social buzz

Very explicit dip coverage — Zacks: 'McDonald's Stock Down 23% in 6 Months: Should Investors Buy the Dip?'; MarketBeat: 'MCD Stock Is Deeply Oversold: Is It Time to Buy the Dip?' (~30% implied upside, 3.2% dividend); TipRanks: 'McDonald's Stock Looks Like a Buy after the Dip.'

Risks & what would break the thesis

U.S. guest counts (especially low-income) haven't stabilized — an affordability headwind management itself acknowledged; GLP-1/wellness trends are a structural overhang; $229 support and the 2022 low (~$217) are must-hold levels, with Nov 4 earnings the decider.

🔬 Research verdict

HOLD deep-researched 2026-10-25 · Dip Top 50 #12

McDonald's is cheap for a reason that hasn't finished playing out: at ~18x forward earnings it trades well below restaurant peers and yields ~3%, but US comparable sales were slightly negative in July–August and management guided Q3 comps slightly negative, with the entry-level value fix not yet launched in the US. The Nov 4 print — and whether September comps actually turned positive as the CFO projected — is the whole debate; until traffic inflects, the dip case is unproven rather than wrong.
